# Legitimate interests assessment (LIA)

> **Draft — not legal advice.** Prepared 29 October 2026 for owner review and sign-off by {{OPERATOR_NAME}}. Uses the ICO's three-part test (purpose, necessity, balancing). Review when the processing changes, when a complaint or objection suggests a problem, and at least every 12 months.

This record covers the processing that the [Privacy Notice](privacy-notice.draft.en.md) bases on legitimate interests. Analytics events are **not** covered here: they rely on consent.

## Context common to all purposes

- Fancy is a free service for adults (18+) in London who host or join small group meals (2–6 people) with strangers. No payments pass through Fancy.
- Members give their information directly, sign in on purpose, and expect the service to keep them safe and to work properly.
- Fancy is run by one person. Data is stored in Seoul (UK adequacy regulations for the Republic of Korea, 2022). No data is sold or shared for advertising.
- Members can object at any time by emailing {{SUPPORT_EMAIL}}; objections are handled under Article 21.

## 1. Sign-up source, sign-up platform and profile completion date

| Test | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Know how many people join, which platform they use, which campaign or shared meal link brought them, and where sign-up stalls, so a solo operator can spend limited time and money on what works and fix broken steps. This is a legitimate business interest and also benefits members (a working sign-up, more meals near them). |
| **Necessity** | Only coarse labels are stored: `ios_app`, `meal_link`, or a short `utm` campaign label (source:medium:campaign); the platform of the first sign-in session; and the date the profile was first completed. No device identifiers, cookies, fingerprinting, third-party trackers or IP-based location. Reports show totals only. Consent-based analytics cannot answer this reliably because members who decline would disappear from basic counts, and sign-up totals are needed regardless. There is no less intrusive way to count sign-ups by source. |
| **Balancing** | Low impact: the data is not sensitive, not used to make decisions about individuals, not shared, and is shown only as aggregates in the staff back office. Members would reasonably expect a service to know how it was found. The label is erased when the account is deleted. The Privacy Notice states it plainly and members can object. Children are not the audience (18+). |
| **Outcome** | Legitimate interests applies. Safeguards: coarse labels only, aggregates only, erased on deletion, raw analytics events kept 13 months (owner approved 29 October 2026), back-office snapshots 48 hourly then daily for 120 days. |

## 2. Optional product survey and private meal scores

| Test | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Learn whether meals went well and what to improve, and spot hosts or tables where members repeatedly have a poor experience. |
| **Necessity** | Answering is optional. Private scores are never shown to the other member and are used in aggregate. No free-text answers are required. |
| **Balancing** | Members choose to answer and expect feedback to be used to improve the service. Scores are not published and do not on their own lead to account action; safety concerns go through reporting. |
| **Outcome** | Legitimate interests applies. Keep scores private and aggregated; any future use for automatic decisions about members needs a new assessment. |

## 3. Safety, moderation and keeping safety records

| Test | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Protect members who meet strangers offline: handle reports, limit or remove content, suspend accounts, enforce blocks, prevent abuse, and meet Online Safety Act 2023 duties. |
| **Necessity** | Reporters' text, the reported content and staff notes are needed to decide a case fairly. Automatic limits are reversible; suspensions are decided by a person. Safety records are kept 12 months after the case closes or the account is deleted (whichever is later) so a member cannot erase evidence by deleting their account and repeated abuse can be recognised. They are visible only to staff handling the case, and the profile photo is not kept. |
| **Balancing** | Members expect a meeting service to act on reports. The impact on a reported member is significant, so safeguards matter: human decisions for suspension, a reconsideration route by email within 30 days, minimal context shown to staff, no report text in alerts or digests, time-limited retention. |
| **Outcome** | Legitimate interests applies, together with the Online Safety Act duties. Review the retention period against Ofcom record-keeping guidance before launch. |

## 4. Service operations from server records

| Test | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Run the service: weekly totals of meals published, applications, meals held and attendance; delivery of sign-in codes and emails; abuse rate limits. |
| **Necessity** | These figures come from records the service already needs to work. They are counted, not profiled. |
| **Balancing** | Low impact; aggregated; members expect a service to keep operational counts. |
| **Outcome** | Legitimate interests applies. |
