# Validation and Decisions

## Critical assumptions

Prioritize assumptions whose failure would change the solution or investment, considering uncertainty, impact and testing cost. Separate demand, payment, delivery and growth; one experiment cannot validate the entire chain.

Record the hypothesis, population, existing evidence, baseline, observable behavior, metric/denominator, decision criteria, resources, risk, result and resulting decision. Label thresholds derived from operating constraints or judgment rather than calling them industry standards.

## Experiments and evidence

Select interviews, prototypes, manual fulfillment, real offers, paid pilots or controlled experiments to fit the claim. Interviews reveal reasons; actions test willingness to bear cost. One payment does not establish recurring value.

Include refusals, failures, cancellations and exits. Avoid selecting only successful customers or founder-influenced participants. Repeat purchase needs an opportunity window and complete denominator. Small samples require uncertainty and limitations, not a product-market-fit claim.

Human research must meet applicable ethics, privacy and informed-consent requirements. Separate research consent from service purchase. For academic experiments consider preregistration, power, randomization, clustering, contamination and multiple testing. A commercial pilot is not automatically a thesis experiment; published associations do not prove a proposed intervention works.

## Distinct paths

| Path | Evaluation |
|---|---|
| Scalable venture | Demand/payment, repeatable delivery, market, unit economics, capital efficiency and risks |
| Small or service business | Reachable customers, labor return, cash flow and operator goals |
| Bounded experiment | Affordable reduction of consequential uncertainty with stopping conditions |
| Research | Literature gap, answerable question, valid method, ethics, sample access and potential contribution |

Paths may coexist, but their evidence is not interchangeable. Lack of venture funding potential does not imply no business value. Commercial failure does not create research contribution. A paper or product does not guarantee admission or funding.

State the recommendation, evidence for/against it, main unknown, smallest useful next step and what would change the judgment. Use stage-appropriate expand/maintain/change/pause/stop criteria. Respect user budgets and goals; proposed amounts, timelines and thresholds are recommendations, not approvals.

Completion: readers understand the appropriate level of commitment, its basis, missing evidence and results that would change the path.
