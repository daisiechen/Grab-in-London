# Business Model Design and Assessment

Build a traceable argument for a business decision. Adapt depth to the task and business stage; do not require a full discovery exercise for every follow-up.

## Workflow

1. Establish the decision, audience, stage, scope and deliverable. Read existing business documents and accepted decisions before asking questions. Ask only about missing inputs that materially change the work.
2. Connect **problem and need -> users and value -> market and alternatives -> solution -> revenue and delivery -> economics -> validation and investment decision**. Revisit earlier assumptions when evidence changes. For a narrow request, analyze the relevant modules and state their dependencies.
3. Load only the references needed below. Reuse reviewed evidence after checking freshness and definitions. Missing data remains unknown.
4. Give a judgment, supporting and opposing evidence, conditions and evidence that would change it. Save business-specific facts, assumptions, models and decisions in the corresponding business documents; keep this skill generic.

## Shared constraints

- Separate sourced facts, company claims, personal experiences, inference, model assumptions, observed results and unknowns. Preserve source, geography, period, unit and denominator.
- Derive solutions from needs. Existing software, fashionable technology and available tools do not establish a reason to adopt them. Distinguish user, purchase decision-maker, payer and beneficiary; roles can overlap.
- Explain benchmark comparability. Listed prices are not transactions, registrations are not active users, customer spending is not platform revenue, and association is not causation.
- Assumption-based models test conditions and sensitivity; they are not measured markets, forecasts or proof of success/failure. Identify whether thresholds come from data, operating constraints or recommendations.
- Discussion and research notes may show exploration. Formal deliverables form a coherent argument for readers without conversation history.
- Analysis does not authorize outreach, human experiments, publication, third-party installation or service changes. Check legal, regulatory and research requirements for the actual jurisdiction and role.
